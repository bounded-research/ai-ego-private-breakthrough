# Proposed experiment and falsifiable predictions

**Status:** research proposal, not preregistered, funded, run or validated. No synthetic or observed results accompany it. This experiment is distinct from the personal evaluation work mentioned in the essay.

## Question

Does personalized AI assistance produce an excess of perceived originality over independently assessed originality, and does this occur alongside convergence across independent users?

## Distinct predictions

1. **Convergence:** given matched tasks, assisted solutions cluster more closely in methods than unaided solutions. This prediction is weakened by equal or greater conceptual diversity with adequate precision.
2. **Miscalibration:** affirming assistance increases perceived novelty more than blinded expert-rated novelty, compared with neutral assistance. Confidence increases accompanied by comparable novelty gains do not support this prediction.
3. **Personalization:** longer personalized interaction increases this calibration gap under matched model, task, time and competence conditions. No interaction or a smaller gap weakens it.
4. **Mitigation:** evidence-seeking assistance reduces the gap without merely reducing all confidence or degrading solution quality. If it only discourages users, that is not the predicted benefit.

Neither convergence alone nor confidence alone confirms the combined hypothesis. A causal claim about assistant behavior requires randomized conditions; correlation between overlap and confidence is insufficient.

## Suggested design

Recruit participants spanning relevant expertise and assign several technical problems with externally assessable solutions. Record expertise, prior familiarity and initial confidence. Randomize unaided, neutral, evidence-seeking and affirming conditions, holding model/version, access, task time and tool budget constant where possible. Define personalization independently of praise; otherwise their effects cannot be separated.

Collect solutions and perceived originality before showing participants external comparisons. Assess method-level overlap using preregistered semantic and expert measures; lexical overlap alone is inadequate. Have independent reviewers blinded to condition assess novelty against a documented prior-work search, correctness and usefulness. Report disagreements and inter-rater reliability. Conceal conditions from analysts where practical.

Specify the primary outcome, calibration scale, minimum effect of interest, multiplicity handling, attrition rules and power analysis before collecting confirmatory data. Choose a sample size from those inputs, not from a desired significant result. Archive null and adverse results. Ethical review, consent and debriefing are necessary for any intentionally affirming or misleading interaction.

## Competing explanations

- The task has only a few good solutions; similar answers may reflect competence.
- Shared prompts, retrieval sources or domain conventions drive convergence independently of praise.
- Human unaided solutions are already clustered.
- Experienced participants correctly recognize familiar work; expertise moderates the effect.
- Assistance produces genuine novelty or improves implementation despite conceptual overlap.
- Selection and survivorship favor public breakthrough claims; private unsuccessful projects are invisible.
- Experts and semantic measures can miss real novelty or mistake cosmetic differences for originality.

## Interpretation

An adequately precise null or opposite result challenges the corresponding prediction. An underpowered null is inconclusive. Report task/model specificity and avoid extrapolating a laboratory effect to prevalence among all AI users. The strongest result would connect randomized behavior to calibrated judgments while measuring convergence separately; it would still not establish a population-wide epidemic.
