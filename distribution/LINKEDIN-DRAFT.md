I've been thinking about a question that grew out of my work with AI: how many people are having private conversations that make similar ideas feel uniquely their own?

In one brainstorming experiment, nine ChatGPT-generated toy ideas had the same name: Build-a-Breeze Castle. That means nine ideas—not nine confirmed independent participants.

A repeated toy name is a small thing. But what happens when we're developing software, exploring research questions or building a business, and the same assistant helps shape the idea and tells us how original it is?

I wrote The Illusion of the Private Breakthrough to explore that gap between personalized encouragement and independent evidence.

The essay draws on research into idea diversity, sycophancy and self-assessment. It also looks at evidence of genuine AI-assisted discovery. The combined phenomenon is a hypothesis, not a proven epidemic, and I outline an experiment that could challenge it.

I'd welcome criticism from people working on human-AI interaction, evaluation and AI-assisted creativity. What prior work am I missing? What would make the proposed experiment more informative?
